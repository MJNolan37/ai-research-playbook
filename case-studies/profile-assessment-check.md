# Checking an AI-generated profile assessment

*A short example, from my own working notes, November 2026.*

I used LinkedIn's AI career assistant (beta) to assess my profile. Before relying on what it told me, I checked three things.

**The rubric.** The assistant gave me two scores at different points, the second higher than the first. They read like a before and after. They weren't. The scores came from two different assessments that measured different things. They could not establish improvement, so I didn't treat them as evidence of any.

**The arithmetic.** One scoring sheet's maximum points summed to 110, while its total was reported out of 100. I did not use that sheet to compare versions.

**The coverage.** In the session where the assistant audited the profile, it could not retrieve the Experience descriptions or the project entries. I recorded that audit as partial. This describes that session; it is not a statement about what the tool can do in general.

## The lesson

Check the rubric, the arithmetic and the coverage before relying on an AI assessment. An AI-generated score is rubric-dependent; it isn't a validated measure of improvement. And an audit that covered part of the page is a partial audit, however confident the summary sounds.
